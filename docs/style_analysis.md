# Style analysis – reference video (`reference/style_reference.mp4`)

Reference: 48 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264, no audio track. It's a paper-collage ad for an unrelated fitness app. This analysis covers only its **format** (look, layout and motion); none of its branding, logo, characters or copy is reused.

Frames studied: 192 frames at 4 fps, 1 fps contact sheets (2 × 24 tiles), 6 fps motion sheets around the hook (0–4 s), the scissors/transform beat (9–13 s), a scene change (16–20 s) and a tear transition (30–34 s), plus full-resolution crops of labels and the character.

## 1. Palette logic

Each scene sits on **one flat, muted, slightly desaturated paper colour**. The colour changes with the topic, and the labels on top reuse a small set of accent colours.

Rules I took from it:
- Backgrounds are never pure: they have a mottled paper grain and a darker vignette, so the eye goes to the centre.
- Accent colours are mid-saturation "printed paper" colours, never neon.
- Text is near-black on light strips and cream-white on red or charcoal strips.
- One accent colour usually dominates per scene. For example, red "warning" labels in the hook and the mustard "solution" strip.

## 2. Paper and cardboard textures

- **Background paper**: low-contrast mottling (large soft blotches) plus fine grain. There's a visible vignette, and the texture stays locked to the sheet (it doesn't boil).
- **Label paper**: each strip has its own faint grain and slightly uneven colour.
- **Other paper types**: lined notebook paper (blue rules, red margin line), sticky-note yellow, receipt/list paper with a torn top edge, calendar cards, and brown kraft/cardboard (the shopping bag) with visible fibres.
- **Stamps**: rubber-stamp ink (red or green outline text with broken, speckled ink coverage) that is slightly rotated.

## 3. Torn-edge labels

- Most labels are **strips torn on all four sides**. The long edges are fairly straight with fine jaggedness, and the short ends are rougher.
- Torn edges show a **thin pale fibrous rim** (the white paper core) around the coloured face. This is the strongest "real paper" cue.
- Labels are rotated between about −6° and +6° and **overlap each other**, stacking into piles with newer labels on top.
- Size encodes importance: big shouty strips for the main claims, small strips for background noise.

## 4. Tape pieces

- Semi-transparent beige/cream masking tape with **zig-zag cut ends**, placed diagonally over the corners of cards and devices.
- A faint striped texture, and the colour of the surface below shows through (about 75 % opacity).

## 5. Drop shadows

- Soft, short, warm-dark shadows offset slightly down and right (about 4–10 px at 1080 p), with a blur of about 10–20 px.
- Shadows grow and soften while an element is "lifted" (during entrances or while it's carried) and tighten when it lands. This is what makes the pieces read as physical.

## 6. The cut-out character

- A **paper doll** built from flat shapes with no outlines: round head, rounded torso, separate upper/lower arms and legs.
- **Brass split-pin fasteners** (gold dots with a highlight) at the shoulders, elbows, hips and knees. The limbs rotate around those pins.
- Minimal face: dot eyes, a short line mouth, blush circles. Expression changes (neutral, O-mouth surprise, smile, frown) are simple shape swaps.
- The same character is reused across scenes with different poses (standing, shrugging, lifting, pointing, waving from behind a card), which gives the ad continuity.
- A subtle paper grain on every part, plus a soft shadow.

## 7. Typography

- **Headlines**: a very heavy high-contrast serif (Didone-like black weight), sentence case, ending with a period ("Real meals."), on off-white, mustard or red torn strips. There's also a bold italic serif for secondary lines.
- **Shouty labels**: condensed heavy grotesque in capitals ("MORE CARDIO").
- **Accents**: a monospace typewriter font for small labels and a handwritten script for annotations ("your plan" with a drawn arrow).
- **Small text / captions**: clean bold sans-serif (Inter-like) in white on a charcoal torn strip in the lower third.
- In this project: *Playfair Display Black / Black Italic* (headlines), *Anton* (big numbers and shouty labels), *Inter* (captions and small text), *Courier Prime Bold* (typed notes), *Caveat Bold* (handwriting). All are SIL OFL fonts, embedded locally.

## 8. Scene-colour changes

- Each topic gets its own background colour: beige, sage, mustard, blue, salmon, then dark.
- The colour changes as a **physical paper action**, not a crossfade:
  - a new sheet slides in from the side with a torn leading edge (16–17 s, beige to sage);
  - a sheet drops down from the top with a torn bottom edge (33 s, blue to salmon);
  - an opening black sheet tears away upwards to reveal the first scene (0–0.3 s).

## 9. Motion

- **Springy pop-in with overshoot**: labels arrive from about 130–150 % scale at partial opacity (as if dropped towards the camera), then slam down to 100 % with a small bounce and a settling rotation. The stagger between labels is about 0.2–0.4 s.
- **Stacking**: new labels land on top of older ones, and the pile grows during the hook.
- **Slight rotation**: every element rests at a small angle and keeps a tiny idle wobble.
- **Parallax**: foreground elements drift slightly more than the background, with a very slow camera push.
- **Elements carry across and transform** instead of hard cuts: the label pile collapses into a T-shirt, scissors cut it into a plan, the shirt pieces fly off-screen as the next scene arrives, and the phone stays while the background changes.
- **Handwriting draws on** stroke by stroke ("your plan" and its arrow).
- **Stamps thump on** with a small scale overshoot.
- **Counters and lists** fill in item by item (calendar weeks flip like cards).
- **Confetti**: gold paper confetti flutters down on the final card, with small pieces tumbling at varied speeds.
- Nothing is ever fully static: something is always breathing, drifting or blinking.

## 10. What this project reuses (format only)

- The torn-strip headline system with a pale fibrous rim, tape, stamps, handwriting annotations, and a charcoal caption strip in the lower third.
- One scene colour per chapter: beige for the hook, blue for the study, mustard for the numbers, salmon for the barriers, sage for the good news, and cream/dark for the call to action.
- A reusable paper-doll character with brass fasteners, in new clothes (a teal hoodie) and with a new design.
- Pop-in springs with overshoot, idle wobble, parallax drift, carry-over elements between scenes, torn-sheet wipes and confetti.
- None of the reference's app, logo, product UI, wording or colours-as-brand.
